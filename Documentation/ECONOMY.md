# OneLife economy — revision 1, save version 33

This specification supersedes the abstract-money model and its $50,000 cash ceiling. Amounts are fictional game-balance dollars, not a model of any jurisdiction. Age Up automatically settles one year. Financial hardship changes circumstances; it never directly ends a life. Health recovery remains separate.

## Ownership and accounting

`EconomyPolicy` authors dollar calibration, taxes, rates, salary bands, asset conversions, and amortization. `EconomyCalculator` is a pure annual calculation shared by forecasts and settlement; forecasts do not consume randomness. Actual settlement supplies realized freelance earnings and rent. Finance owns cash, unsecured debt, tax reconciliation, retirement, and payment allocation. Assets owns properties, mortgages, rental state, and receipt application. The simulator sequences the domains.

Cash is a nonnegative whole-dollar integer. Monetary rates round to the nearest dollar. Swift checked arithmetic and explicit overflow checks reject unsupported values rather than wrapping. Unpaid compulsory bills become identified liabilities. Optional purchases require cash. Mortgage principal declines only when a payment receipt supplies principal actually paid. Capitalized interest can take a loan above the original purchase price; normalization must preserve it.

The completed-year ledger distinguishes gross work and partner earnings, support/pension, rent, prior withholding, tax reconciliation, essential payments, property operations, interest, principal, retirement transfers, unpaid obligations, and cash movement. Principal/interest subtotals explain payments and must not be added again. Retirement contributions and draws move wealth between accounts. Restricted tenant deposits represent matching restricted funds and liabilities, excluded from available cash and net worth; only actual damage can consume them.

The [monetary-reference inventory](Working/ECONOMY_MONEY_INVENTORY.md) is regenerated by `python3 Scripts/audit_economy.py` and includes Swift and bundled content.

## Assets and debt

The six properties cost $150,000, $225,000, $325,000, $900,000, $1.8 million, and $3 million in catalog order. Mortgages require 20% down, use a fixed 6% rate and 30-year schedule, and calculate twelve internal monthly payments per annual settlement. Qualification requires total scheduled debt payments below 43% of gross household income; only 70% of previously realized rental income qualifies. Property operations cost 2.5% of current value annually: 1.5% tax/insurance and 1% routine maintenance. Amenities and restoration have separate prices.

Gross market rent is 6% of property value before strategy, vacancy, tenant, and world modifiers. The interface reports net rental cash flow after costs. Existing resale discounts and bounded appreciation remain. Sales and foreclosure deduct debt, operating arrears, and closing costs; net equity is credited and a deficiency remains a consumer liability.

Original movable prices convert by these factors: cars ×50, helicopters ×300, jewelry ×10, abstract firearms/upgrades ×5; light/twin/jet aircraft ×50/150/1,000; fishing/speed/cabin/yacht boats ×20/30/100/1,000. Those assets do not currently add annual ownership bills. The existing 55% vehicle total-loss settlement is an abstract catastrophic-loss payout, not an insurance premium, policy, or claims system.

Criminal operational assets are a separate layer from the personal portfolio. Criminal equipment, safehouses, and front businesses remain in the criminal-path state because they change operation risk, concealment, planning, storage, heat, and organization cash flow. They are managed from the Crime surface and do not become personal resale inventory. Personal firearms and vehicles can still create legal or notoriety exposure when owned, but the Crime system does not duplicate their ownership, condition, sale, or RNG state.

Unsecured rates are student 6%, consumer 18%, medical 0%. Required payments are accrued interest plus 10% of opening principal, capped at the balance owed. Extra payments target consumer, medical, then student debt. Unpaid interest stays owed; unpaid principal is never deducted.

Annual allocation order is tax reconciliation, essentials, primary housing, unsecured minimums, secondary property obligations, discretionary spending, then retirement. Missed required property payments drive delinquency. Four years can trigger foreclosure. Primary housing loss uses temporary accommodation at 60% of rent, with receiving-domain health/relationship pressure; stable cash flow restores ordinary renting.

## Debt relief

After two consecutive shortfall years, consumer plus medical debt above annual gross household income can qualify for a five-year workout. The review lists exact asset sales, initial debt payment, cash retained, and included debt remaining before acceptance. Entry sells secondary property and nonessential movable assets, preserving the primary home and cheapest car. Cash above one month of essential household/housing costs pays included debt.

During the plan, included interest freezes, purchases and luxury upgrades pause, retirement contributions stop, and cash above a three-month essential buffer pays included debt after essentials and required secured/student payments. After five completed years remaining consumer/medical debt is forgiven; student and secured debt survive. A ten-year cooldown follows completion. Life Review records the workout. Recovery exposes at most three contextual actions: reduce spending, sell/downsize, and negotiate relief.
